What goes wrong

Three things that quietly cost ophthalmology practices money

Ophthalmology has the second-best economics in healthcare search. The cost is almost always a routine-exam and surgical-consult budget fighting each other.

👁

Routine exams and surgical consults in one campaign

Someone searching for an annual eye exam and someone comparing cataract surgeons are worth completely different amounts. One campaign means the cheap, high-volume exam searches out-compete the expensive, high-value surgical ones for the same budget.

Surgical consults are worth 10–50x a routine exam
💰

Underbidding LASIK and cataract terms

These are the highest-value conversions in the specialty, and the click costs reflect it. An account sized for routine-exam CPCs gets outbid on exactly the searches worth winning.

Elective surgical terms run well above the specialty average CPC
📞

Optometry booked by phone, budgeted by form

Many optometry patients call to check insurance and availability before booking online. If call tracking isn't wired in, the account is optimising against a partial picture of what's actually converting.

A meaningful share of optometry bookings start as a call

How we build it

How an ophthalmology account should be structured

Live in three business days, then reviewed every working day after that.

1

Routine and surgical run as separate campaigns

Different budgets, different bids, different landing pages. A $4 routine-exam click and a $40+ LASIK-consult click should never be competing for the same daily budget.

2

Bid to match conversion value, not conversion volume

A cataract or LASIK consult is worth many multiples of a routine exam. The bidding is set so the account chases the appointment worth winning, not just the one that's cheapest to get.

3

Call tracking wired in from day one

So insurance and availability calls — which optometry gets more of than most specialties — count as the conversions they are, instead of vanishing from the account's data.

4

A landing page per intent group

Routine exam, urgent/diagnostic, and elective surgical each get a page that answers what that specific searcher actually wants to know before booking.

5

Then reviewed every working day

Elective surgical terms move in cost quickly and healthcare ad copy gets disapproved for wording nobody expected. Both are caught the same day, not at a monthly review.

FAQ

Ophthalmology & optometry advertising questions

What does an ophthalmology or optometry lead cost on Google Ads?

+

The US median is $30.88 per lead at $4.32 per click and a 14.0% conversion rate — the second most efficient of sixteen healthcare specialties benchmarked, behind only dermatology.

Should routine eye exams and LASIK/cataract surgery share a campaign?

+

No. They're worth very different amounts and attract different searchers. Running them together usually means the cheaper, higher-volume exam searches consume the budget meant for the far more valuable surgical consults.

Why do LASIK and cataract terms cost more per click?

+

Because the value of winning that patient is much higher than a routine exam, so more practices bid harder for it. An account that bids the same across all of ophthalmology will consistently lose the surgical-consult auctions it most wants to win.

How much should an ophthalmology practice budget for Google Ads?

+

At $30.88 per lead, $1,500 a month should produce roughly 48 enquiries in an average market — though a practice pushing elective surgical consults should expect a smaller number of far more valuable leads rather than optimising for lead count alone.
