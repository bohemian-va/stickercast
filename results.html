What actually happened

An independent family practice runs a walk-in clinic one day a week, on Sundays. The ads only run that day, so four Sundays of advertising is four days of data — not four weeks, not a month. We are counting days, not calendar pages.

Over those four days: $185.72 of ad spend, 44 clicks, and 8 patient enquiries at $23.22 each. That is a 18.2% conversion rate against a US healthcare median of 8.09%.

The change was not that more patients arrived. It was that, for the first time, the practice could say how many and what each one cost.

Read this before you read anything above.

This is one client account, not a portfolio. Forty-four clicks is a small sample — small enough that ordinary variation moves the percentages a lot. One of the four days produced six of the eight enquiries; strip that day out and the conversion rate falls to 3.2%. We expect the number to settle well below 18% as volume accumulates, and we are showing you the day-by-day table so you can see that for yourself rather than taking the average on trust.

There is also no before-and-after here. Before this campaign, the practice had no conversion tracking. The doctor knew patients found him through Google because he asked them. Enquiries were very likely happening in earlier months too — nobody was counting them. So we are not claiming we increased anything. What changed is that the number became measurable.

The real problem

Most practices can't tell you what their ads did last month.

Not because the ads failed. Because nobody wired up the measurement. The front desk knows patients mention Google. The doctor hears it at the appointment. But no one can put a number on it, so every budget decision after that is a feeling.

That is the first thing we fix, and usually the only thing that needs fixing before the account starts making sense. Optimising a number you cannot see is guesswork with a report attached.

FAQ

Why only four days of data?

+

Because that is honestly all there is. This client advertises on Sundays only, so four Sundays is four days of advertising. We would rather show you a small real number than a large vague one.

Is an 18.2% conversion rate normal?

+

No. The US healthcare median is 8.09%. A rate above 18% on 44 clicks is a small sample and should be read that way — one good Sunday produced six of the eight enquiries. We expect the figure to settle lower as volume grows.

Can you show a before-and-after?

+

No, and we won't pretend otherwise. Before this campaign the practice had no conversion tracking at all. The doctor knew patients came from Google because he asked them at the desk. There is no honest baseline to compare against, so we are not going to manufacture one.

How many clients do these numbers come from?

+

One. Stickercast is a small operation and we are not going to aggregate a single account into a claim about an industry.

What does this actually demonstrate?

+

Measurement. Before, this practice could not tell you how many patients the ads produced or what each cost. Now it can: 8 enquiries, $23.22 each, on $185.72 of spend. That is the change — from unknown to known.
