What happened

An independent family practice runs a walk-in clinic one day a week, on Sundays. The ads only run that day — so four Sundays is four days of advertising, not four weeks.

Over those four days: $185.72 of ad spend, 44 clicks and 8 patient enquiries at $23.22 each. That is an 18.2% conversion rate against a US healthcare median of 8.09%, at roughly a third of the median cost per lead.

What changed was not that more patients arrived. It was that, for the first time, the practice could say how many and what each one cost.

This is one client account and 44 clicks — a small sample, and day three produced six of the eight enquiries. FAQ

About these numbers

Why four days and not a month?

+

Because this client advertises on Sundays only, so four Sundays is four days of advertising. We are counting days the ads actually ran rather than calendar pages.

Is an 18.2% conversion rate normal?

+

No, it is well above the US healthcare median of 8.09%. It is also drawn from 44 clicks, which is a small sample, and we expect it to settle lower as volume builds.

How many clients does this come from?

+

One. We are a small operation and we are not going to present a single account as an industry-wide claim.

What does this actually show?

+

That the account became measurable. Before this campaign the practice knew patients came from Google because the doctor asked them at the desk, but nobody could say how many or what each one cost. Now it is 8 enquiries at $23.22 each.
