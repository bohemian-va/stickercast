Where the budget leaks

Three things that quietly cost dental practices money

01

You're paying $7 a click for emergency traffic you can't serve

"Emergency dentist" and "tooth pain" searches spike after hours and on weekends. If your ads run when your phone goes to voicemail, you're funding your competitor's new patient. Ad scheduling has to match when someone actually answers.

02

High-value and low-value procedures share one budget

An implant consult and a teeth-cleaning enquiry are worth wildly different amounts to your practice, but a single 'dental' campaign bids the same for both. Split by procedure value or the cheap enquiries will quietly eat the budget.

03

Your new-patient offer is doing the work your landing page should

Most dental ads point at a homepage. The patient lands, can't find the offer that made them click, and leaves. At $7.03 a click that's an expensive bounce — and it drags your Quality Score down, which raises the price of every future click.

Compliance is built in, not bolted on

Dental advertising is regulated province by province — RCDSO in Ontario, the provincial dental colleges elsewhere. Rules around before-and-after imagery, guarantees, specialist titles and fee advertising are strict and actively enforced. Campaigns are vetted against them before launch.

FAQ

Dental advertising questions

What does Google Ads cost for a dental practice?

+

Benchmark data puts general dentistry at $7.03 per click with a 7.74% conversion rate, giving an average cost per lead of $84.77 USD. Emergency dentistry runs $7.85 per click and orthodontics $8.76 — among the highest in all of healthcare. Most practices need $1,500–$4,000 per month in ad budget to see consistent flow.

Dental clicks are expensive. Is it still worth it?

+

It depends entirely on your case values. At $84.77 per lead and a 40% booking rate, a new patient costs roughly $210 to acquire. Against a cleaning that's marginal. Against an implant, Invisalign case or full restorative plan, it's trivial. The campaign has to be built to chase the second kind.

Should I advertise emergency dental?

+

Only if someone answers. Emergency searches convert at 8.89% and carry real urgency, but they cluster outside business hours. If those calls go to voicemail you're paying $7.85 a click to send patients elsewhere. We set ad scheduling around your actual answering capacity.

How do you handle dental college advertising rules?

+

Every campaign is checked against your provincial college's standards before it runs — that covers fee advertising, guarantees, specialist designations and before-and-after imagery. These rules differ by province and are actively enforced, so this isn't a box-ticking exercise.
