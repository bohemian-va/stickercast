Getting clicks but no bookings? 7 reasons med spa Google Ads don't convert

Stickercast · October 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer: A good click-through rate with no bookings almost always points to what happens after the click, not the ad. The usual culprits: a generic landing page, no conversion tracking, slow follow-up, a form that asks too much, paying for the wrong searches, a poor mobile experience, and not enough trust signals on the page.

We've seen it on campaigns of our own: a click-through rate well above average, a few dozen clicks, and not one form submission. When that happens, the ads are usually doing their job. The problem is everything after the click.

Here are the seven causes we see most often, roughly in order of how much they cost.

1. Ads send people to your homepage

Someone searches for laser hair removal, clicks your ad, and lands on a homepage listing twelve treatments. They have to hunt for what they came for, and many don't bother. Industry data cited by Web Tonic suggests a dedicated treatment landing page converts at around 8–15%, compared with 2–4% for a generic services page.

Fix: one landing page per treatment you advertise, with the treatment in the headline and the booking form visible without scrolling.

2. You're not tracking conversions, so you can't see what works

If form submissions and calls aren't tracked as conversions in Google Ads, Google's bidding can't tell a valuable click from a useless one, and neither can you. You may also be getting enquiries that you're simply not counting.

Fix: track every form submission and click-to-call as a conversion, and test it by submitting the form yourself before launch.

3. Follow-up is too slow

Someone fills in your form at 8pm. You call back the next afternoon. By then she's booked somewhere else. Aesthetic clients often contact several clinics at once, and the first to respond properly has a real advantage, as The Valley Marketing Group points out.

Fix: decide who calls new enquiries back and how fast. Offer online booking where you can, and make sure calls during busy treatment hours don't go straight to voicemail.

4. The form asks for too much

Date of birth, full address, treatment history, how did you hear about us… every extra field loses people. For a first enquiry you need a name, a phone number and perhaps the treatment they're interested in.

Fix: cut the form to three or four fields. Collect everything else at the consultation.

5. You're paying for the wrong searches

Look at your search terms report and you'll often find clicks from people searching for training courses, injector jobs, at-home devices, day spas or cheap deals. They click; they'll never book. Benchmarks suggest med spa accounts commonly waste a quarter or more of their budget on irrelevant searches (source).

Fix: build a negative keyword list before launch (jobs, course, training, DIY, at home, free) and review search terms every few days at the start.

6. The page is slow or awkward on a phone

Most people searching for treatments are on their phones. A page that takes several seconds to load, a form that's fiddly to fill in, or a phone number that isn't tappable all quietly lose bookings. Page speed also feeds into Google's Quality Score, which affects what you pay per click.

Fix: open your landing page on your own phone on mobile data. If it's slow or frustrating, it's costing you.

7. Nothing on the page builds trust

Aesthetic treatments are a considered decision. People want to know who will treat them, whether they're qualified, and roughly what it costs. A page with no injector names, no credentials, no reviews and no pricing guidance gives them every reason to keep comparing.

Fix: add your practitioners and their qualifications, real reviews from your Google Business Profile, starting prices or a clear consultation price, and what happens at a first appointment. Common questions

Why do my med spa Google Ads get clicks but no bookings?

Usually because of what happens after the click: a generic landing page, slow follow-up, a long form, untracked conversions, irrelevant searches, a poor mobile page, or missing trust signals.

What conversion rate should a med spa landing page get?

Industry figures suggest dedicated treatment landing pages convert around 8–15% of visitors, compared with 2–4% for generic services pages.

How quickly should a med spa follow up on a Google Ads lead?

As fast as you reasonably can, ideally the same day and within business hours. Many people enquire with several clinics at once.
