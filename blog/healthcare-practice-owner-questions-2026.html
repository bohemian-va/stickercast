5 Questions Healthcare Practice Owners Are Asking in 2026

September 2026 · 8 min read · Healthcare Marketing

If you own an independent healthcare practice, you're likely asking some version of these five questions right now. Not because you're behind the curve — because these are the real, persistent problems that keep practice owners up at night.

1. "How do I get consistent new patients when patient acquisition costs keep rising?"

This is the question we hear most often. Patient acquisition costs are climbing across all specialties, competition for ad space is intensifying, and many practices find themselves spending more on marketing to get the same number of enquiries they got two years ago.

What makes this worse: nearly 40% of new patient enquiries still come from phone calls, but most healthcare marketers lack the tools to track them accurately. That means your actual cost per patient is invisible — you're making budget decisions on incomplete data.

The practices doing well aren't spending more on ads. They're spending smarter: separating walk-in/urgent traffic from routine appointments, bidding differently for high-value procedures versus general enquiries, and tracking phone calls alongside form submissions so the full picture is finally visible.

2. "Is Google Ads even worth it for our practice size?"

Many smaller independent practices wonder if they're too small for paid search to make sense. The math on this has actually improved in the last two years.

A dermatology practice running ads on a $1,500/month budget in an average market should expect roughly 80 enquiries at the US median of $18.54 per lead. A physical therapy clinic at the same spend gets roughly 45 leads. Even a dental practice — where leads run $84.77 each — can work the numbers if the account is structured right.

The real question isn't "Are we big enough?" It's "Are we tracking this correctly?" Most practices that think Google Ads doesn't work for them never installed proper conversion tracking. They're bidding on phantom data.

3. "Why is my reimbursement rate dropping while my overhead keeps climbing?"

This one affects everyone. Reimbursement rates have become the single biggest pressure on independent practices — 68% of practice owners cite low reimbursement as a primary threat. Meanwhile, staffing costs (50%), overhead (49%), and administrative burden are all climbing.

This is actually why patient acquisition matters more now than it ever has. You can't negotiate reimbursement rates with payers, but you can control your patient flow. When you can acquire patients at a known, fixed cost, suddenly your unit economics become predictable. A practice that can reliably generate 80 new patients a month at $23 per enquiry has leverage — they know their revenue floor.

4. "How do I know if my current marketing agency is actually working?"

One of the most revealing questions comes down to a simple one: Can your agency tell you what your cost per lead actually is?

If they can't — or if the number seems disconnected from your gut sense of what's happening in the clinic — you have a tracking problem. This isn't a criticism of the agency. It's a structural issue: healthcare ads run across multiple channels, conversions happen by phone and by form, and many practices have never installed the tracking infrastructure to connect the dots.

The practices with the clearest picture of ROI are the ones running ads inside their own Google Ads account (not an agency-owned account they can't access), with proper phone tracking installed, and with someone reviewing the account daily instead of monthly.

5. "Can I actually afford to own this practice independently, or am I going to get acquired?"

This is the existential question. Between regulatory mandates, reimbursement pressure, and the operational complexity of running a small business in healthcare, many practice owners genuinely wonder if staying independent is still viable.

The answer isn't "Yes, it's easy." It's "Yes, if you're smart about the business side."

Most physicians didn't go to medical school to learn payer enrollment, claims denial management, lease math, and technology decisions — but those are the decisions that determine whether you survive year three of ownership. Patient acquisition is part of this equation. A practice that can reliably convert enquiries into patients has predictable revenue and better cash flow. That's not just a marketing problem; it's a business survival problem.

The common thread: Most of these questions come down to visibility. Practice owners need to know what their numbers actually are — cost per lead, cost per patient, which channels work, which don't. Once you have that clarity, the decisions become easier.

What actual practice owners are doing about it

The practices moving the needle in 2026 are the ones that made three specific decisions:

First: They installed proper tracking on day one, including phone call tracking. No more guessing.

Second: They separated their campaigns by type of patient. Walk-in/urgent gets its own budget and ad copy. Routine appointments get a separate campaign. High-value procedures (if applicable) get their own campaign. This alone cuts wasted spend by 15–25% in most practices.

Third: They moved away from monthly reviews and toward daily account oversight. At $7 a click in dental or $5 a click in general healthcare, a bad keyword can burn through your budget fast. Catching it in a day versus a month makes the difference between break-even and profit.

The bottom line

The environment in 2026 is harder for independent practice owners than it was five years ago. Reimbursement is tighter, competition is fiercer, and overhead is higher. But the practices thriving are the ones that stopped treating patient acquisition as a cost center and started treating it as a business lever.

You can't control payer rates. You can't control the regulatory environment. But you can control whether you know what each new patient actually costs. And that clarity is worth more than any percentage increase in ad spend.
