What goes wrong

Three things that make dental accounts expensive

At $7 a click, what you exclude matters as much as what you bid on.

💰

Paying implant prices for cleaning enquiries

'Dentist near me' brings in everything: a cleaning, a lost crown, someone comparing implant quotes. All at the same click cost. Without separating them, your most expensive traffic subsidises your least valuable appointments.

Implant terms can exceed $15 a click
⏰

Missing the emergency searches

Emergency dental searches convert far better than anything else in the specialty, and they happen outside office hours. If the campaign isn't scheduled and the phone isn't answered, that traffic goes to whoever picks up.

Highest-converting dental intent by a distance
💳

No mention of cost or financing anywhere

Dental is the specialty where price is the objection. Ads and pages that avoid the subject lose the click to whichever competitor addresses it — even when the competitor is more expensive.

The most common reason a dental click doesn't book

How we build it

How a dental account should be structured

Live in three business days, then reviewed every working day after that.

1

Procedures get their own campaigns and budgets

Implants, ortho, emergency and general each run separately. It is the only way to stop a $15 implant click and a $4 cleaning click sharing a budget and a landing page.

2

Emergency runs on its own schedule

Evenings and weekends, with the call routing to somebody who answers. Emergency is where dental's dismal conversion average gets beaten, but only if the phone is live.

3

An aggressive negative keyword list

Free, cheap, dental school, DIY, and the long tail of price-shopping terms. At $7 a click, money saved on negatives is usually larger than money saved on bid tuning.

4

Cost addressed on the page, not avoided

Financing options, insurance accepted, what a consult costs. Answering the price question keeps the expensive click from bouncing to a competitor who answered it.

5

Then reviewed every working day

At $7 a click a bad keyword costs real money fast. Daily review is worth more in dental than in any other specialty we work in.

FAQ

Dental advertising questions

What does a dental lead cost on Google Ads?

+

The US median is $84.77 per lead at $7.03 per click and a 7.7% conversion rate. General dentistry is among the more expensive healthcare specialties to advertise in, well above the $66.02 healthcare median.

Why is dental advertising more expensive than other specialties?

+

Density and lifetime value. There are more dental practices bidding in a given area than almost any other specialty, and a patient who stays is worth thousands over time, so practices bid aggressively. Both forces push click costs up.

Which dental keywords actually produce patients?

+

Emergency terms convert best by a wide margin, followed by specific procedure terms like dental implants and Invisalign, and by insurance-specific searches. Generic terms such as 'dentist near me' are expensive and mix high and low value intent in one keyword.

How much should a dental practice budget for Google Ads?

+

At $84.77 per lead, $2,000 a month produces roughly 23 enquiries in an average market. Dental is a specialty where a larger budget on a badly structured account loses money faster, so the structure has to come first.

How fast can a dental campaign go live?

+

Three business days from account access, and the first month of management is free — which in dental matters more than most, because it is the specialty where a bad month costs the most.
