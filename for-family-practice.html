What goes wrong

Three things that quietly cost family & primary care practices money

Primary care covers same-day sick visits, routine physicals, chronic care management and more, often in one practice. Treating all of it as one search intent is the most common mistake.

📆

Same-day and preventive care in one campaign

Someone searching for a same-day sick visit and someone booking a routine annual physical are in completely different moods, with completely different urgency. One campaign, one set of ad copy, and the urgent searcher usually leaves for whoever answers that urgency directly.

The most common structural gap we see in primary care accounts
📞

Booked by phone, tracked as a form (or not at all)

Family and primary care patients call to check same-day availability and insurance more than almost any specialty we work with. If those calls aren't tracked, a large share of what the campaign actually produced is invisible to the account.

Calls are typically the majority of primary care enquiries
❓

No answer to "do you take my insurance"

It's usually the first question, and if the ad or page doesn't address it, the click leaves to go find a practice that does — even one that's otherwise a worse fit.

The most common reason a qualified click doesn't book

How we build it

How a family & primary care account should be structured

Live in three business days, then reviewed every working day after that.

1

Same-day and preventive care get separate campaigns

Different urgency, different ad copy, different landing pages. A walk-in sick visit and a routine physical shouldn't be competing for the same budget or reading the same page.

2

Call tracking installed before anything else goes live

So the calls that make up most primary care enquiries are counted, instead of the account bidding against a number that's missing most of what actually happened.

3

Insurance and new-patient status answered up front

In the ad and on the landing page, not three clicks deep. It's usually the first thing a prospective patient wants to know.

4

Conversion tracking installed from day one, even if nothing existed before

The Mayland account below had no tracking before this campaign — the change wasn't more patients, it was being able to say how many and at what cost, for the first time.

5

Then reviewed every working day

So a wasted search term or a broken tracking tag gets caught the same day, not discovered a month later.
