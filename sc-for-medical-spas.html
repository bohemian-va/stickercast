Where the budget leaks

Three things that quietly cost medical spas practices money

01

Naming the treatment gets your ad disapproved

Google restricts advertising of prescription drugs. Put a well-known injectable brand name in a headline and the ad gets limited or rejected — often silently, so you assume the campaign is just underperforming. Advertising the outcome instead of the drug clears the policy and usually converts better anyway.

02

You're competing with clinics that discount on price

A race to the bottom on per-unit pricing attracts the patient who leaves for whoever is $20 cheaper next month. Campaigns built around consultations and treatment plans attract the patient who stays.

03

Aesthetic demand is seasonal and your budget isn't

Enquiries spike before wedding season and the December party stretch, and go quiet in between. A flat monthly budget underspends the peaks and wastes money in the troughs.

Compliance is built in, not bolted on

Aesthetic advertising sits under Health Canada guidelines, provincial college standards where a physician is involved, and Google's healthcare and medicines policy — which is the one that actually stops ads running. Prescription treatment names, before-and-after imagery and outcome claims all carry restrictions. Every campaign is vetted before launch.

FAQ

Medical Spas advertising questions

What does Google Ads cost for a medical spa?

+

There's no single medspa benchmark, but the two closest specialties bracket it: dermatology averages $4.90 per click and $18.54 per lead, while plastic and cosmetic surgery averages $5.75 per click and $102.51 per lead. Where you land depends on treatment mix — routine aesthetic services behave like dermatology, surgical and high-ticket work behaves like cosmetic surgery.

Why do my med spa ads keep getting disapproved?

+

Almost always prescription drug names. Google restricts advertising of prescription treatments, so naming a well-known injectable brand in a headline or description triggers a limited or disapproved status. The fix is to advertise the outcome and the consultation rather than the product — which clears policy and typically converts better.

Can I use before-and-after photos in my ads?

+

Carefully, and often not in the ad itself. Google's healthcare policies restrict certain imagery, and your provincial college may have its own rules where a physician is involved. We keep those out of ad creative and handle them properly on the landing page where the rules differ.

How do you handle seasonality in aesthetics?

+

Budget follows demand rather than the calendar. Enquiries climb ahead of wedding season and the December social stretch and fall away between. Campaigns get paced up before those peaks and shifted toward maintenance and retention treatments in the quiet months.
