How much should a med spa spend on Google Ads, and what should a lead cost?

Stickercast · October 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer: Published 2026 benchmarks put med spa clicks at roughly $5–$28 depending on the treatment, and well-run campaigns at about $30–$80 per lead (US data, USD). Most single-location clinics need around $1,500–$3,000 a month in ad spend to get enough leads to learn from. Work out your own budget backwards from how many new clients you want and what one is worth to you.

“How much should we spend?” is the first question almost every clinic owner asks, and “it depends” isn't a useful answer. Here's what published benchmarks say, and a simple way to work out a number for your own clinic.

What med spas pay per click

Cost per click depends mostly on the treatment and how competitive your city is. Ranges cited in The Valley Marketing Group's 2026 roundup:

Search typeTypical cost per click (USD)
Neurotoxin / anti-wrinkle “near me” and city searches$12–$28
Lip and dermal fillers$10–$22
Body contouring$8–$18
Laser hair removal near me$6–$15
“Med spa” (general)$3–$10

Other sources land in a similar place. Web Tonic's 2026 statistics cite an average of $5–$15 per click for med spa keywords, with very competitive terms like “med spa near me” going above $25.

What a lead should cost

A “lead” here means a form submission or phone call from someone interested in booking. The spread is wide, and campaign setup explains most of it. The same Valley Marketing Group article puts the industry average at $120–$200 per lead, but says well-optimized campaigns typically get leads at $30–$80. Workee quotes $25–$75 per lead for med spas generally.

The gap between a $40 lead and a $180 lead usually comes down to three things: which searches you pay for (and which you block), whether the ad goes to a treatment-specific page, and how fast your team follows up.

How much budget you need

Published guidance for a single-location med spa in a mid-size market starts around $1,500–$3,000 a month in ad spend, with $3,000–$6,000 suggested for very competitive cities (source). Below roughly $1,500 a month, the main problem isn't that ads don't work. It's that you get so few leads each week that you can't tell what's working.

A smaller budget can still work if it's focused. Run one or two treatments in one area, rather than spreading it across everything you offer.

Work out your own number in four steps

Instead of starting with a budget, start with what you want out of it. Here's an example with made-up numbers; swap in your own.

  1. New clients you want per month: say 10.
  2. Booking rate: what share of enquiries actually book? Benchmarks cited by Web Tonic put Google Ads leads at roughly 40–60% booked. Use 40% to be safe, so you need 25 enquiries.
  3. Cost per lead: assume $60 to start. 25 × $60 = $1,500 a month in ad spend.
  4. Check it against client value. If a new injectables client typically rebooks several times a year, compare $150 per new client ($1,500 ÷ 10) to what that client is worth over twelve months, not just the first visit.

If the maths works at a conservative cost per lead, the budget is worth testing. If it only works at the very best-case number, start smaller and focus on one treatment.

Don't forget the management fee

Ad spend goes to Google. Management is separate. Agencies charge in different ways: a percentage of ad spend, a flat fee, or a setup fee plus a retainer. A flat fee is easier to plan around, and it doesn't reward anyone for simply spending more of your money.

Common questions

What is a good cost per lead for a med spa on Google Ads?

Published 2026 benchmarks suggest well-optimized med spa campaigns get leads at around $30–$80 (USD), with poorly set up campaigns often paying $120–$200.

What is the minimum Google Ads budget for a med spa?

Most guidance suggests around $1,500 a month in ad spend for a single location. Smaller budgets work best when focused on one or two treatments.

Are Google Ads costs in Canada the same as the US?

Not necessarily. Most published benchmarks are US-based and in USD. Canadian costs vary by city and competition, so treat US figures as a starting reference, not a quote.
