What goes wrong

Three things that quietly cost dermatology practices money

⚖

Medical and cosmetic in one campaign

Someone searching for a mole check and someone searching for laser resurfacing have nothing in common except your building. Same campaign means one budget, one set of ad copy and one landing page trying to speak to both. Neither gets spoken to well.

The single most common structural fault we see
💰

Bidding against med spas on cosmetic terms

On terms like botox and laser hair removal you are in an auction with med spas that have far looser margins and will outbid you all day. Your advantage is that you are a physician's practice — the ads have to say so, or the click is wasted.

Cosmetic CPCs run 40–70% above medical derm
📅

No way to tell a consult from a click

Dermatology books through calls as often as forms. If call tracking isn't wired in, half the conversions simply don't exist in the account, and the bidding algorithm optimises against a number that's missing its better half.

Calls are typically 45–60% of derm enquiries

How we build it

How a dermatology account should be structured

01

Medical and cosmetic get separate campaigns

Different budgets, different ad copy, different landing pages, different conversion values. A skin cancer screening and a filler consult are two businesses sharing a waiting room, and the account should reflect that.

02

Condition-level keywords over specialty keywords

Bidding on 'dermatologist' puts you against everyone. Bidding on 'eczema specialist' and 'adult acne treatment' puts you in front of people who already know what they need, at a lower cost and a higher conversion rate.

03

Call tracking before anything else

Numbers that record which campaign produced each call, so the half of your enquiries that arrive by phone stop being invisible to the account.

04

Landing pages per condition

One page per condition group, each answering the questions that actually precede booking: waiting time, whether it's covered, what happens at the first visit.

FAQ

Dermatology advertising questions

What does a dermatology lead cost on Google Ads?

+

The US median is $18.54 per lead at $4.90 per click and a 25.3% conversion rate, making dermatology the most efficient of sixteen healthcare specialties benchmarked. Cosmetic dermatology terms cost substantially more than medical terms, often 40 to 70 percent higher per click.

Should medical and cosmetic dermatology run in the same campaign?

+

No. They attract different patients, convert at different rates and carry very different click costs. Running them together means one budget and one set of ad copy serving two audiences, and the cosmetic terms will usually consume the budget while the medical terms convert better.

Can dermatology practices advertise cosmetic treatments on Google?

+

Yes, with limits. Google restricts some claims and before-and-after imagery in healthcare advertising, and prescription treatment names carry their own rules. We build the ads within those constraints rather than getting them disapproved and rewriting later.

How much should a dermatology practice budget for Google Ads?

+

At $18.54 per lead, a budget of $1,500 a month should produce roughly 80 enquiries in an average market. What matters more is how many new patients you can actually absorb — we'd rather set the budget to your capacity than to a round number.
